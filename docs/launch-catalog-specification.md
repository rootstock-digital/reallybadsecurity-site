# RBS launch catalog specification

**Status:** proposal for owner review. Nothing in this document is approved, published, or configured in Shopify.

**Scope:** proposed initial merchandise catalog for the isolated Shopify test store. It does not authorize product, collection, metafield, theme, app, fulfillment, pricing, or integration changes.

## Brand and content model

Really Bad Security (RBS) is a personality-led commentary and culture brand for the owner’s observations and humor about security, AI, technology, creator life, and internet work. Its voice is sharp, technically literate, and slightly sarcastic—not a cybersecurity services-company voice and not generic slogan merchandise.

The RBS site is the home and commentary/content hub. A social post can become a short editorial entry, collection note, or design rationale when it has lasting context; social remains the fast conversational layer. Shopify is the supporting commerce channel: it owns products, pricing, inventory, cart, checkout, orders, policies, and fulfillment. The RBS site owns commentary, editorial, and brand context, and should lead readers to relevant Shopify collections without duplicating commerce operations.

### Brand filter

Proposed merchandise is in scope when it is intentional and recognizably RBS:

- It is sharp, technically literate, and slightly sarcastic.
- It comments on security, AI, technology, creator life, or internet work through a specific observation rather than a generic catchphrase.
- It has a point of view that could plausibly come from the owner’s commentary.
- It does not make unsupported security, compliance, privacy, performance, availability, or product claims.
- It does not borrow vendor names, product UI, logos, proprietary copy, or a recognizable third party’s trade dress.

Security-native AI humor remains welcome, especially where it references data handling, verification, permissions, or real workflows. AI and creator/internet-work humor do not need an explicit security reference, but must remain specific, intentional, and recognizably RBS rather than generic “AI powers,” motivational, or engagement-bait merchandise.

All design copy, artwork directions, formats, blank choices, colorways, and price inputs below are **proposals requiring owner approval**.

## Proposed design concepts

### 1. Least Privilege Club

- **Collection:** Core Signals.
- **Front copy/art direction:** Small left-chest lock icon formed from three nested permission brackets, with `LEAST PRIVILEGE CLUB` in compact utility type below it.
- **Back copy/art direction:** Large centered text: `DEFAULT DENY / INTENTIONALLY USEFUL`. Below it, a minimal three-row permission matrix with only the required cells marked.
- **Security concept:** Least privilege and default-deny access control.
- **Practitioner recognition:** It expresses a foundational control that security engineers, IAM administrators, developers, and auditors encounter daily; the permission-matrix treatment keeps the joke grounded in the work.
- **Brand/clearance risks:** Do not reproduce an identifiable cloud-provider IAM policy, vendor logo, UI, or proprietary icon. Have legal/owner review the phrase and final artwork for trademark conflicts.

### 2. One Second, Let Me Ask AI

- **Collection:** AI, Safely.
- **Front copy/art direction:** `ONE SECOND / LET ME ASK AI` in an intentionally utilitarian prompt-window treatment; use abstract interface lines only.
- **Back copy/art direction:** `DRAFTING IS NOT DECIDING` with a small checklist: context / source / judgment.
- **Commentary concept:** The everyday habit of reaching for AI while preserving human judgment and context.
- **Audience recognition:** Technically literate AI users, builders, and internet workers recognize both the useful shortcut and the habit of outsourcing too much thought to it.
- **Brand/clearance risks:** Do not use AI-provider names, logos, interface likenesses, or imply any tool is unsafe or authoritative. Owner/legal review must confirm the final wording remains commentary rather than a product claim.

### 3. Published, Not Promoted

- **Collection:** Internet Work.
- **Front copy/art direction:** `PUBLISHED, NOT PROMOTED` set like a small release-status label with a deliberately ambiguous distribution graph.
- **Back copy/art direction:** `THE FEED HAS NOTES` with a minimal, abstract post-to-dashboard trail—not a recreated social-platform interface.
- **Commentary concept:** Creator/internet-work humor about making work visible without pretending the distribution system is predictable.
- **Audience recognition:** Creators, operators, and online workers recognize the gap between publishing work and it being surfaced.
- **Brand/clearance risks:** Do not name or mimic a platform, claim suppression, or promise reach. Ensure the humor is about the experience, not an allegation about a particular algorithm.

### SKU rules

- `RBS` is the brand prefix.
- The next segment is the stable design code: `LPC`, `OSAI`, or `PNP`.
- The format segment is `TEE`, `HOOD`, `HAT`, or `MUG`.
- Color uses a controlled short code such as `BLK`, `BONE`, `CHAR`, `GRPH`, `NVY`, `OLV`, or `WHT`.
- Size uses the supplier-approved size code (for example, `S`, `M`, `L`, `XL`, `2XL`).
- Supplier variant IDs must be stored in an operational field or fulfillment mapping, never substituted for the RBS SKU.

## Controlled taxonomy proposal

### Product types

Use only these customer-facing product types for launch:

- `Tee`
- `Hoodie`
- `Headwear`
- `Mug`

### Vendor convention

Set every launch product vendor to `Really Bad Security`. The fulfillment provider is operational data and must not replace the RBS vendor identity.

### Controlled tags

Tags are operational and collection-supporting metadata, not keyword stuffing. Apply only controlled values in these groups:

- `audience:security-tech-culture`
- `lens:security`, `lens:ai`, or `lens:creator-internet-work`
- `collection:core-signals`, `collection:ai-safely`, or `collection:internet-work`
- `format:tee`, `format:hoodie`, `format:headwear`, or `format:mug`
- `drop:launch-01`
- `fulfillment:printful` only after that provider is owner-approved and connected
- `status:sample-approved` only after a documented sample approval

### Required `rbs.*` metafields

| Metafield | Proposed validation type | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rbs.design_name` | single-line text; controlled design name | Connects formats belonging to one design. |
| `rbs.design_code` | single-line text; pattern `^[A-Z]{3,6}$` | Stable internal design code used in SKU governance. |
| `rbs.commentary_lens` | single-line text; enum: `security`, `ai`, `creator-internet-work` | Supports collection logic and editorial linking. |

Validation details must be confirmed against the Shopify test store’s available metafield-definition controls before implementation.

### Collection membership logic

- **Core Signals:** manual or controlled-tag collection where `collection:core-signals` is present. Initial member: Least Privilege Club.
- **AI, Safely:** manual or controlled-tag collection where `collection:ai-safely` is present. Initial member: One Second, Let Me Ask AI.
- **Internet Work:** manual or controlled-tag collection where `collection:internet-work` is present. Initial member: Published, Not Promoted.
- **New Drop:** controlled tag `drop:launch-01`; remove only through owner-approved drop retirement.
- **Apparel:** product type is `Tee` or `Hoodie`.
- **Desk / Field Gear:** product type is `Mug` at launch; expand only when a new approved type exists.

Use manual collections during test-store setup if that is simpler to audit. Automate only after tags and metafields are proven consistent.

## Product approval checklist

Each product and each variant must remain draft/unpublished until all applicable checks have a recorded owner approval.

- [ ] The design passes the RBS brand filter: sharp, technically literate, slightly sarcastic, intentional, and recognizably RBS rather than generic slogan merchandise.
- [ ] If the design uses a security, privacy, compliance, or performance reference, the wording does not make an unsupported claim.
- [ ] Artwork, copy, typefaces, icons, and visual references have copyright, trademark, and brand-clearance review; no vendor UI, logo, or proprietary policy text is copied.
- [ ] Final production artwork meets the supplier’s file, color, contrast, and print-area requirements.
- [ ] The selected Printful blank, colorway, size range, print method, embroidery scale, and placement are documented and owner-approved.
- [ ] A physical sample or documented test order is reviewed for fit, construction, print quality, color accuracy, packaging, delivery timing, and damage handling.
- [ ] SKU, supplier variant mapping, product type, vendor, controlled tags, and required `rbs.*` metafields are complete and internally consistent.
- [ ] Price inputs have been reviewed: blank cost, decoration cost, fulfillment cost, shipping subsidy, payment fees, expected returns/replacements, tax treatment, and target gross-margin percentage. Final selling prices require separate owner approval.
- [ ] An inventory/availability rule is documented, including the source of truth, out-of-stock behavior, supplier discontinuation handling, and who monitors exceptions.
- [ ] Fulfillment window, shipping regions/rates, return/cancellation/replacement policy, and order-support owner are approved and match customer-facing content.
- [ ] Product images include every required view, accurately represent the deliverable item, and contain accessibility-ready alt text.
- [ ] PDP keyboard flow, variant selection, image gallery, color contrast, form errors, cart handoff, and checkout handoff have passed accessibility review in the test store.
- [ ] Product is reviewed in the isolated test store before any production recreation or website integration is authorized.
